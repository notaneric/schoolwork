The five big ideas from Oct 23

  • VSEPR: electron groups around the central atom repel and spread out as far apart as possible. That separation predicts the shape.
  • Electron-pair geometry is where all the electron groups are (bonds and lone pairs). Molecular geometry is where the atoms are. They match only when the central atom has no lone pairs.
  • Steric number (SN) = atoms bonded to the central atom + lone pairs on it. A double or triple bond counts as one group.
  • Lone-pair effect: a lone pair takes more space than a bonding pair, so it usually squeezes the bond angles below the ideal. The angle is guaranteed ideal only with no lone pairs on the central atom. (Linear and square planar keep 180° and 90° because the lone pairs sit symmetrically.)
  • SN 5 and 6 placement: with SN 5, lone pairs and multiple bonds go in the equatorial positions first. With SN 6, a second lone pair goes opposite the first.

How to name a shape in four steps

  • Draw the Lewis structure and find the central atom.
  • Count groups around it: each bonded atom is 1 (single, double or triple), each lone pair is 1. That total is the SN and gives the electron-pair geometry.
  • Split it into BP and LP and read the molecular geometry off the table.
  • Angle: ideal if LP = 0. With lone pairs it is usually "less than" the ideal, except the symmetric shapes: linear stays 180° and square planar stays 90°. When unsure, copy the angle from the table.
